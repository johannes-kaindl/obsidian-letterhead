# Explanation — DIN 5008

DIN 5008 is the German standard of "writing and layout rules for text and
information processing" (*Schreib- und Gestaltungsregeln für die Text- und
Informationsverarbeitung*). For business letters it specifies, among other
things, where the address field, the fold marks and the writing margins go — so
that a folded letter sits correctly in a **window envelope (DIN long)** and can
be processed by machine.

The DIN theme implements these measurements (revision of the standard:
**March 2020**) exactly, as CSS tokens. That way the recipient address falls
precisely into the envelope window.

## Form A vs. Form B

The difference is the height of the letterhead — and thus how far everything
below it shifts down.

| Element | Form A | Form B |
|---------|--------|--------|
| Letterhead height (top margin) | 27 mm | 45 mm |
| Fold mark 1 (from top) | 87 mm | 105 mm |
| Hole mark (from top) | 148.5 mm | 148.5 mm |
| Fold mark 2 (from top) | 192 mm | 210 mm |
| Writing margin left / right | 25 mm / 20 mm | 25 mm / 20 mm |
| Address field | 85 × 40 mm | 85 × 40 mm |

**Form B** is the usual choice when there's a logo/letterhead at the top (more
room). **Form A** is more compact. Switchable in the settings.

## Why the values are sensitive

The address has to appear in the envelope window after folding. If you shift
`--bk-din-address-top` or the margins, it may no longer fit — which is why these
tokens are marked *DIN-critical* in the [theming reference](../reference/theming.md).
Typography, colours and spacing, by contrast, are free.

If your envelope deviates slightly: first try the **Print offset top (mm)**
setting (shifts the content downwards, fold marks stay put); for anything else,
fine-tune individual geometry tokens in the **Custom CSS** field and check with
**Open letter preview**.

## Print margins vs. DIN positions

Printers cannot print borderless, and without page margins multi-page letters
break right at the sheet edge. The plugin therefore always prints with fixed
`@page` margins: page 1 top 10 mm (the DIN letterhead sits high by design),
continuation pages top 25 mm, bottom 20 mm everywhere. The DIN measurements above
are unaffected — all positions are defined **from the paper edge**; internally
the components subtract the page margin again.
